AppealMate

"You've been denied. Now what?"

A voice-first agent that helps Medicare Advantage beneficiaries appeal denied prior authorizations — by simply talking.

The problem

~80%

of Medicare Advantage prior-auth denials that reach appeal are overturned — but almost nobody appeals.

1 page+

denial letters full of plan jargon: "not medically necessary," "criteria not met," codes and clause numbers.

65+

the typical caller: managing multiple conditions, stressed, and expected to master CMS form fields and clinical-policy citations.

60 days

the redetermination window — ticking from the date on the letter, whether or not the member understands it.

The appeal is winnable. The paperwork is the barrier.

Why an agent, and why voice

  • One button. Speak. Done. No forms, no portals, no jargon — designed for an older demographic first, not as an afterthought.
  • Aria introduces herself first — name, purpose, and a promise: everything stays private, and nothing is filed without your explicit say-so.
  • The agent does the hard part: matches the story to the plan's own clinical policy criteria and drafts the argument with the highest chance of overturn.
  • Evidence-linked, not vibes: every drafted argument cites the specific policy criteria the denial fails to satisfy.
  • ElevenLabs voice is the interface itself — for a caller who can't manage forms, voice isn't a feature, it's the door.

How it works

1 · Meet Aria

She introduces herself, explains she's on your side, and asks for your name.

2 · Tell your story

One question at a time: your ID, the service, the date, the reason in the letter.

3 · She checks the policy

Your story is matched against clinical policy criteria — the plan's own rules.

4 · She drafts, you decide

Aria reads the appeal back to you. Say "file it" — or ask for changes. Nothing moves without your voice.

The safety design

  • She refuses to file a weak appeal. If the policy doesn't support overturning, Aria says so plainly and files nothing — no wasted 60-day window on a doomed appeal.
  • Read-back before filing. The entire appeal is spoken aloud for approval. "File it" is the only trigger; anything else holds.
  • Say "stop" at any time. It works at any point in the conversation, immediately.
  • Append-only audit trail. Every step — question asked, policy matched, refusal, filing — is logged and inspectable.
  • Honest evidence: run counts show drafted / refused / filed out of total sessions. No invented success rates.

Live demo — three real scenarios

Heart CT scan

Denied "not medically necessary — stress test first." But a prior stress test was inconclusive and the patient can't exercise. CPB 0228 says that's exactly when CCTA is indicated.

Extra physical therapy

Denied at a "session cap." CPB 0325 says therapy is medically necessary to restore function after surgery — and two falls last month with documented balance deficits meets them.

Power wheelchair

Denied because "a scooter is enough." She can't transfer safely and has hand tremors — CPB 0271 says that's exactly when a power wheelchair is indicated.

In each case: the plan's denial contradicts the plan's own policy. That's the argument Aria finds and files.

Honest limits

  • Policy criteria are verbatim excerpts from real Aetna Clinical Policy Bulletins (CPB 0228, 0325, 0271), fetched from Aetna's public clinical-policy pages. This is demo content, not clinical or legal advice.
  • Three scenarios in the demo library; real deployment needs the full CPB set.
  • An appeal is not an approval: we measure drafts and filings, not overturns. Overturn rates cited here are published industry figures, not our measured results.
